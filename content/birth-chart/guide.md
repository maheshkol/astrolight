# How to Read a Birth Chart

A birth chart is easiest to understand when you separate **where something is** from **what an astrology tradition says it means**.

AstroLight follows this basic sequence:

1. Convert the birth date and time to a precise astronomical time.
2. Calculate planetary positions.
3. Convert positions into zodiac coordinates.
4. Calculate the Ascendant and house cusps from location and time.
5. Identify aspects and, in Vedic mode, sidereal signs and Nakshatras.
6. Interpret those coordinates using traditional astrology.

## Start with the three anchors

### The Sun

The Sun's zodiac sign is commonly used as the "Sun sign." It is one part of the chart and is traditionally associated with identity, vitality and purpose.

### The Moon

The Moon moves much faster than the Sun and is therefore more sensitive to birth time. Traditional astrology associates it with emotional patterns, habits and instinct. In Vedic astrology, the Moon's sidereal position determines the Janma Nakshatra.

### The Ascendant

The Ascendant, or rising sign, is the zodiac degree rising on the eastern horizon at the birth location and time. Because Earth rotates continuously, the Ascendant can change substantially over a day. Accurate time and location therefore matter.

## Then read the houses

Houses provide a second coordinate system. In AstroLight's current equal-house model, House 1 begins at the Ascendant and each following house begins 30° later.

A planet combines with its house to create a traditional interpretation: the planet represents a function or theme, while the house describes the area in which that theme is emphasized.

## Finally, look at aspects

An aspect is an angular relationship between two planetary longitudes. A conjunction is near 0°, an opposition near 180°, a square near 90°, a trine near 120° and a sextile near 60°.

The exact separation and the chosen orb matter. AstroLight displays the angular relationship so the reader can see the basis for the interpretation.

## Vedic mode

Vedic mode uses sidereal longitude rather than tropical longitude. AstroLight applies a Lahiri-style ayanamsa approximation, then maps the Moon's sidereal longitude into one of 27 Nakshatras and four padas.
